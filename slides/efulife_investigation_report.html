Question 3

Did Cybernet's claimed July 27 fix actually change anything?

Method: checked two independent historical BGP archives, RIS (RIPE) and RouteViews (University of Oregon, unrelated organization), for what the internet's routing tables actually said before and after the claimed date.

RIS · 4,342 events, July 1–Aug 15

Every path ends ..., 9541, 141008, before and after. No Pakistani ISP newly appears as a Cybernet peer around the date.

RouteViews · snapshots, July 20 vs Aug 3

Downloaded full routing tables, parsed with bgpdump. All 17 distinct paths seen: byte-for-byte identical on both dates.

No evidence of a fix, from either archive. Live measurement five weeks later still shows the identical hairpin.

One real change was found, just not this one

Before July 27, GSL Networks had a direct peering session with Cybernet. After, that direct session is gone, the indirect route through Omantel is unchanged. A real infrastructure change, an international backbone adjustment, unrelated to domestic Pakistani reachability.

Why is PTCL slower today (203ms) than in the archived measurement (119ms)?

The BGP path is proven identical, so it isn't a routing change. Comparing every hop's RTT pins the entire increase to one exact spot:

That hop's own address has no fixed identity (see Q2), consistent with the same announced path now landing on different physical hardware.

Question 4

Is this EFU Life specific, or a Cybernet-wide pattern?

Method: EFU Life isn't technically "a Cybernet prefix", it owns its own address block under its own ASN, with Cybernet only as transit. Checked Cybernet's own 957 directly-announced blocks (regular customers without their own ASN) instead, for whether any Pakistani ISP shows up as a direct BGP neighbor.

A contradiction turned up first, and it's worth walking through because the resolution matters. A 5-block spot check found 3 blocks with zero visible domestic peering, including the block containing the gatekeeper router itself, the same IP that 8 of 9 live probes reached domestically in Question 1. BGP said "international only." Traceroute, real measured packets, said otherwise.

The resolution: RIS and RouteViews only hear announcements from networks that plug directly into their own collectors, overwhelmingly large international carriers. A small, purely-domestic Pakistani link can be completely real and completely invisible to these archives at the same time, not a contradiction, a blind spot. PTCL shows up because it's large enough that some internationally-visible network likely sees a path through it somewhere; smaller domestic-only ISPs mostly can't get that same incidental visibility.

So a "0" from this method means "no domestic peer visible from these vantage points," not "no domestic peer exists." Ran the full check anyway, treating every "0" with that caveat attached, across all 952 remaining blocks:

PTCL is the only Pakistani ISP that ever appears, in any of the 952 blocks. A real, substantial domestic connection exists, dominating some blocks entirely, but it evidently doesn't carry Cybernet's whole address space. EFU Life's block falls on the excluded side, along with Cybernet's own core infrastructure block. Whether that's a deliberate filter, a commercial tier distinction, or historical inconsistency isn't answerable from routing data alone.

Where this leaves things

Summary

  • EFU Life is single-homed to Cybernet, confirmed by both live traceroute and two independent BGP archives.
  • Non-Cybernet ISPs reach it only via an international hairpin through GSL Networks and a Zain Omantel block, and this is not PTCL-specific, at least 4 distinct ISPs show the identical chain.
  • No evidence in RIS or RouteViews that Cybernet's claimed July 27 fix changed anything. The path measured today is the same path, and PTCL's RTT got worse, not better.
  • Cybernet does peer domestically with PTCL for a real share of its own address space (at least 30.6% of blocks checked), just not for EFU Life's, or its own core block.
  • Several city-level geolocation claims in the earlier write-up didn't survive re-verification and were corrected at the source.

Open questions

  • Why do EFU Life's block and Cybernet's own core block fall outside the PTCL peering session while ~31% of other blocks don't?
  • What's behind Nayatel's unexplained slow-but-domestic Globalping reading (113ms, no foreign hop, same day RIPE Atlas Nayatel probes read 24–26ms)?
  • Only one RouteViews collector (route-views2) was checked; its other collectors could add independent vantage points.
