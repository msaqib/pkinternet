EFU Life: One Door, Everyone Else Flies Abroad

Re-checking the tromboning case study live, and testing Cybernet's claimed July 27 fix

efulife.com · AS141008 · hosted in Karachi, single-homed to Cybernet (AS9541)

Background

Why this site

  • Dr. Saqib flagged EFU Life as unusual: routing "trombones" from different ISPs, and is sometimes local
  • An earlier case study (Exp 07 panel data) found every ISP funnels through one Cybernet hop, no matter the route
  • Task: re-check this live, right now, with every currently-connected probe
  • Second task: Cybernet claims it fixed the routing on July 27, 2026. Check RIS and RouteViews for whether that's true

Methodology detour

Don't trust a "country" label

  • Three hops on PTCL's path were originally labeled Muscat, Los Angeles, and UAE
  • Checked each one's own reverse-DNS hostname and its real neighbors
  • Muscat, retracted: the neighbors in that block span Ashburn, Adelaide, Seattle, Phoenix
  • Los Angeles, retracted: never a PTR finding at all, an unverified geolocation-database guess
  • Singapore, held up: its own hostname confirms it, unchanged from July to today
  • UAE → Oman: RIPE's own registration record says country: OM directly. City still unconfirmed, not Muscat

Lesson: an ASN's registered country is paperwork about the company, not proof of where a router physically sits. Only a descriptive hostname on the exact IP, or the registry's own field, counts as evidence.

The July 27 question

Did Cybernet's claimed fix actually happen?

RIS (RIPE's BGP archive)

  • 4,342 announcement events, July 1 – Aug 15
  • Every path ends ..., 9541, 141008, before and after
  • No Pakistani ISP newly appears as a Cybernet peer around the date

RouteViews (independent archive)

  • Full routing snapshots, July 20 vs Aug 3
  • All 17 distinct paths seen: byte-for-byte identical
  • Installed bgpdump, parsed raw MRT files directly

Two independent archives agree: nothing changed. And live measurement today (Sept 1-2) still shows the same hairpin, five weeks later.

One real change found

Something did change, just not this

Before July 27: GSL Networks had a direct peering session with Cybernet.

After July 27: that direct session is gone. The indirect route (GSL → Omantel → Cybernet) is unchanged.

A real, specific infrastructure change, an international backbone adjustment between Cybernet and GSL. Not a domestic fix. Doesn't help any Pakistani ISP.

Why is PTCL slower now than in July?

119ms → 203ms, same path, one exact spot

hop 7  Singapore    119.2 → 85.2 ms   (got FASTER)
hop 8  GSL, unknown 112.8 → 194.6 ms  (+82ms, THE jump)
hop 10 Omantel      114.1 → 197.6 ms  (stays elevated)

Everything before this hop got faster. Everything after carries the same +83–91ms offset. The BGP path is proven identical, so this isn't a route change, it's the same announced path landing on different physical hardware at one specific, identity-unstable address.

Scoping the problem

Is this EFU-specific, or Cybernet-wide?

  • EFU Life isn't technically "a Cybernet prefix", it has its own ASN (141008), Cybernet is only its transit
  • Checked Cybernet's own 957 announced blocks (customers without their own ASN) instead
  • First spot-check (5 blocks): 3 showed zero domestic peering, 2 showed heavy PTCL peering
  • But one of the "zero" blocks contains the gatekeeper IP itself, which 8 of 9 probes reached domestically

Contradiction caught: BGP said "no domestic peer," traceroute proved otherwise. Resolution next slide.

Important correction

BGP archives have a blind spot

RIS and RouteViews only hear announcements from networks that peer directly with their own collectors, mostly large international carriers.

A small, domestic-only Pakistani ISP can have a perfectly real link to Cybernet that never touches anything RIS is listening to. Invisible, not nonexistent.

PTCL shows up because it's large enough that some internationally-visible network likely sees a path through it. Smaller ISPs mostly can't get that visibility, regardless of how real their domestic link is.

EFU Life's own finding is unaffected: it comes from traceroute ground truth (real packets from many ISPs, all converging on one hop), not from BGP visibility alone.

Where things stand

Summary

  • EFU Life is single-homed to Cybernet, confirmed by both traceroute (data plane) and BGP (control plane)
  • Non-Cybernet ISPs reach it only via an international hairpin through GSL Networks (Singapore) and a Zain Omantel block (Oman)
  • That pattern isn't PTCL-specific: 4 of 7 Globalping-checked ISPs show the identical chain
  • No evidence in RIS or RouteViews that Cybernet's claimed July 27 fix changed anything
  • Cybernet does peer domestically with PTCL for a real share of its own address space (roughly 31% of blocks checked), just not for EFU Life's
  • Several city-level geolocation claims from the earlier write-up didn't survive re-verification and were corrected
Open questions

What's still unresolved

  • Why does EFU Life's block, and Cybernet's own core block, fall outside the PTCL peering session while other blocks don't?
  • Is that a deliberate filter, a commercial tier distinction, or historical inconsistency?
  • What's actually behind Nayatel's unexplained slow-but-domestic Globalping reading?
  • Worth extending the RouteViews check to its other collectors, only one was used here
