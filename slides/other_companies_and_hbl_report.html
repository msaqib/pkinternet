Method, and a correction made along the way

The methodological catch

Why we still can't prove PIE itself was used

A router usually has several addresses, one per cable, plus a stable "identity" address unrelated to any of them. When it replies to a traceroute probe, it commonly uses that identity address, not whichever cable actually carried your packet. So a router can genuinely be using its PIE-facing connection and still never reveal that specific address.

What this means concretely: checking whether a traceroute hop falls inside PIE's published peering-LAN address range (58.181.127.0/24) cannot conclusively confirm or rule out exchange usage, in either direction. The DE-CIX route-server data above is the stronger evidence, it comes directly from the exchange, not from guessing at addresses.
Where this leaves things

Summary

  • EFU Life's specific problem does not show up in any of the 8 other Cybernet-connected companies actually tested live.
  • HBL Bank is reached fine by everyone in practice, but Cybernet's own route to it is unexpectedly slow and circuitous, real, repeatable, not yet fully explained.
  • Cybernet is confirmed, directly from DE-CIX's own data, not a member of PIE Karachi's route server. PTCL is.
  • HBL's own address block is live and announced at two international DE-CIX locations, one of them through the identical GSL Networks / Omantel chain found in EFU Life's hairpin.
  • Address-matching against a peering-LAN prefix cannot settle whether a specific exchange was used, a real methodological limit worth carrying into how the paper's own PKIX/PIE crossing claim is worded.
