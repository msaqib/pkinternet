# Findings 4.1 — Small-ISP path-tromboning census (in progress)

**Author:** Rayan Atif
**Source:** Experiment 4.1 (`experiments/04.1_small_isp_tromboning/`). Complete
block-level census of every FLL (small/local) Pakistani ISP: TCP/80 Paris traceroute
to 8 IPs per announced /24, from 7 PK source probes, RTT-physics detector.

Companion to `findings/04_path_tromboning_worldcall.md` (the single-ISP proof of method).

## Status (2026-07-01)

**~53% complete** — **3,190 of 5,976 measurements** (747 blocks × 8 IPs;
each fans to 7 probes). Checkpointed every batch (`results/run_20260627_192918/`),
resumable; the run has been paced by the RIPE 100-concurrent cap and interrupted by
laptop sleeps (each resumed from checkpoint). Numbers below are from the **partial
(~29–49%) data** and are **directional, not final.**

## What the partial data shows

*(from ~6,100 classified traceroutes across 13 of 48 ISPs, 228 of 747 blocks.)*

### 1. Most small-ISP traffic stays local, but a real slice hairpins
Overall **~12% trombone abroad, ~87% local, ~1% inconclusive** — so small PK ISPs are
mostly reachable in-country, but a meaningful fraction hairpins.

### 2. The source ISP dominates the outcome
Trombone rate depends far more on *where you measure from* than on the destination:

| Source probe | trombones |
|---|---|
| **PTCL (Karachi)** | **78%** |
| **Cybernet (Haripur)** | **47%** |
| Nova, Z-Com, Nayatel, Orbit, Cybernet-Khi | **3–6%** |

**PTCL and Cybernet-Haripur send most small-ISP traffic out of the country**; the
others route locally. (This *reverses* the Worldcall RQ4 result where PTCL had the good
route — for Worldcall specifically PTCL peered locally; across small ISPs generally it
hairpins them. Tromboning is a per-(source, destination) property.)

### 3. Exits are China- and US-heavy — not just Singapore
Top foreign exits: **ChinaNet / China Mobile-HK (335)**, **Cogent-US (58)**, Hurricane
Electric-US, Equinix-SG, plus UAE/Oman. Much more scattered than Worldcall's single
Equinix-Singapore exit — reflecting the different transits' international paths. (A large
"?" bucket = real RTT-jump trombones whose foreign hop didn't resolve — fine for the
rate, not for exact attribution.)

### 4. Transit: PTCL is the dominant hairpinner
Where attributable, **PTCL (363)** hands off abroad most, then Cybernet (97), Transworld
(82), Brain Telecom (56).

### 5. Worst-hairpinned destination ISPs (so far)
Fast Web 25% · **Brain Telecom 21%** (large, 2k+ traces) · Sign In 16% · Gerry's 12% ·
Logon 11% · Cube XS 11% · Connect 6%.

### 6. "Per-prefix" is NOT clean (the Exp 04 open question, now measured)
With 8 IPs/block, **intra-block consistency ≈ 66%** — about **1 in 3 (block, source)
pairs have IPs that disagree** (some hairpin, some local within the same /24). Caveat:
the 8 IPs ran seconds–minutes apart, so a "mixed" block may reflect **time-flipping**
(intermittency) as much as true per-host routing — but either way a /24 is **not** a
clean routing atom.

## Caveats

- **Partial + single-snapshot** — 49% done, 13/48 ISPs so far; verdicts flip over time
  (intermittency is pervasive — e.g. Smart Net flipped PTCL 100%↔0% between runs). Quote
  as ranges once the full run + repeat rounds are in.
- PTCL-Khi's 78% is on a smaller sample (it went offline mid-run).
- Readable per-trace hairpin paths with `<<< LEAVES PK` markers (and a `reached=`
  label per trace): `results/run_20260627_192918/routes_tromboning_*.txt`.
- **Target IP (aimed-at) ≠ last responding hop.** We probe 8 *spread* addresses per
  /24 (`.16, .48, …`) as traceroute targets — not necessarily live hosts. Small-ISP
  /24s are sparse, so most targets are dead/ICMP-filtered and never answer: the trace
  ends in `* * *` and the **last responding hop is an upstream/transit router, not the
  target**. Target and last-hop coincide **only when `reached=True`**. This does **not**
  affect the tromboning verdict — the foreign/exit hop appears *before* the target, so
  the hairpin is detected even when the target address is empty (we measure the route
  *toward the block*, not one host's liveness).
