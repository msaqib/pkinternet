# Findings 4.1 — Small-ISP path-tromboning census (complete)

**Author:** Rayan Atif
**Source:** Experiment 4.1 (`experiments/04.1_small_isp_tromboning/`). Complete
block-level census of every FLL (small/local) Pakistani ISP: TCP/80 Paris traceroute
to 8 IPs per announced /24, from 7 PK source probes, RTT-physics detector.

Companion to `findings/04_path_tromboning_worldcall.md` (the single-ISP proof of method).

## Status — COMPLETE (2026-07-03)

**Full census done: 18,260 traceroutes with data.** Targeted **48 ISPs / 747 announced
blocks** (8 spread IPs per block, 7 source probes); usable traces returned for **45 ISPs
/ 696 blocks** (the rest returned nothing measurable). Outputs in
`results/run_20260627_192918/`: `census_20260627_192918.csv` (one row per
source×block×IP), `isp_tromboning.csv` (per-ISP Q1/Q2), `ip_status_table_*.csv`
(ISP→block→IP→probe), `filtered_reached_tromboned.csv`, and the route files below.

## Headline

| Verdict | traces | share |
|---|---|---|
| **Local (stays in PK)** | 15,451 | **84.6%** |
| **Trombone (hairpins abroad)** | 2,002 | **11.0%** |
| Inconclusive | 807 | 4.4% |

**~1 in 9 probes into small-ISP address space leaves the country and comes back.**
Most small-ISP space is reachable in-country, but a solid, non-trivial slice hairpins —
and it is highly concentrated by *who measures from where*.

## 1. The source ISP dominates the outcome

Tromboning depends far more on **where you measure from** than on the destination:

| Source probe | trombone rate |
|---|---|
| **Cybernet — Haripur** | **46.3%** (260/562) |
| **PTCL — Karachi** | **38.4%** (586/1525) |
| Cybernet — Karachi | 10.5% (218/2084) |
| Nova (Transworld-transit) | 7.8% (333/4264) |
| Z-Com | 7.5% (344/4609) |
| Orbit | 6.6% (133/2012) |
| **Nayatel** | **4.0%** (128/3204) |

**Two vantages — Cybernet-Haripur and PTCL-Karachi — send 38–46% of small-ISP traffic
out of the country; everyone else routes mostly local (4–10%). Nayatel is the cleanest**
(most locally-peered). Note the *same* Cybernet ISP tromboning 46% from Haripur but only
10.5% from Karachi — so it is a **per-PoP / per-source-router** property, not just a
per-ISP one. (This tempers the partial-data "PTCL 78%", which was a small early sample.)

## 2. Transit: the two LDIs split the hairpins almost evenly

Where the hand-off abroad is attributable, **Transworld (589) and PTCL (566)** carry it
in near-equal share — together **~58%** of all trombones. Then Cybernet's own backbone
(AS9541, 183) and Cogent (AS174, 105). ~19% of trombones (375) have an unresolved
transit hop but are still RTT-proven. So the hairpinning is overwhelmingly executed by
**the two licensed international operators** — exactly the transit structure the project
predicts.

## 3. Exits are China-heavy, then US, then Singapore

Among the 2,002 trombones, where the foreign exit resolves:

| Exit country | trombones |
|---|---|
| **(unresolved hop, RTT-proven)** | 1,191 |
| **China (CN)** | 347 |
| **United States (US)** | 259 |
| **Singapore (SG)** | 107 |
| UAE (AE) | 35 |
| Sweden / Indonesia / Netherlands | 21 / 13 / 12 |

Far more scattered than Worldcall's single Equinix-Singapore exit (Exp 04) — reflecting
the different transits' international paths. The large unresolved bucket is real RTT-jump
tromboning whose foreign hop didn't answer (fine for the rate, not for exact geo).

## 5. "Per-prefix" is mostly — but not perfectly — clean

With 8 IPs/block, **intra-block consistency = 82%** (2,242/2,724 source×block pairs are
uniform: all their IPs agree local-vs-trombone). So a /24 is a *usable* routing atom
~4/5 of the time, but **~18% of (source, block) pairs split** — some IPs hairpin while
others in the same /24 stay local. Caveat: the 8 IPs ran seconds–minutes apart, so a
"mixed" block may reflect **time-flipping** (intermittency) as much as true per-host
routing. (Better than the partial-data 66% — the fuller sample is more consistent.)

## 6. Reachability & hairpins into the ISP's own network

`reached=True` means **a hop in the destination ISP's own ASN answered** — i.e. the trace
got *into* the target network (not that the exact spread target IP replied; those are
usually dead/filtered, see the caveat). **2,007 of 18,260 (11%)** traces reached the
destination ISP's network. Of those, **325 reached it *via* an international hairpin**
(`reached AND tromboned`): **Brain Telecom dominates (182)**, then Connect (34), Optix
(32), Sign In (25), Multinet (11). These are the cleanest proof cases — the packet
demonstrably enters the destination ISP's network, but only after leaving and re-entering
Pakistan. Rendered in `routes_filtered_reached_tromboned_*.txt`.

## Caveats

- **Single-snapshot.** One census pass; verdicts flip over time (intermittency is
  pervasive). The 11% headline is a snapshot rate — repeat rounds would give a range.
- **Detector is RTT-physics, geo-IP-proof** (foreign hop ≥40 ms, or ≥60 ms jump, or any
  hop ≥70 ms = left PK; <45 ms = local; ignore >500 ms; exclude Shaw AS6327 / in-PK
  Cogent AS174). A "trombone" verdict is real even when the foreign hop doesn't resolve.
- **Target IP (aimed-at) ≠ last responding hop.** We probe 8 *spread* addresses per /24
  (`.16, .48, …`) as traceroute targets — not necessarily live hosts. Most are dead/
  filtered and never answer: the trace ends in `* * *` and the last responding hop is an
  upstream/transit router, not the target. `reached=True` here means the trace reached a
  hop **in the destination ISP's own ASN** (it got into the target network), *not* that
  the exact spread target IP answered — it usually doesn't. This does **not** affect the
  tromboning verdict — the foreign/exit
  hop appears *before* the target, so the hairpin is detected even when the target is
  silent (we measure the route *toward the block*, not one host's liveness).
