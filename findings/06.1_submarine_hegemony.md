# Finding 6.1 — The SMW5 fault in the control plane: rerouting happened above the duopoly, not below it

**Experiment:** `experiments/06.1_submarine_hegemony/` · **Extends:** Exp 06 (same event, data
plane) · **Data:** daily AS-hegemony (IIJ IHR), 15 Jun – 10 Jul 2026, bracketing the 2 Jul fault.

## Headline

> During the SMW5 submarine-cable fault, **domestic networks' dependencies on the two operators
> stayed frozen** — at national scale: **98.5% of the 273 gate-dependent Pakistani networks held
> their majority gate** (4 switched, ~4% re-balanced), and weighted by estimated population
> (Exp 6.1.1, W1) **99.63% of Pakistan's ~42.1M internet users** were on networks whose gate never
> moved — every one of the country's 15 largest ISPs held. Meanwhile **the operators re-carriered
> ~21% (TWA) and ~13.5% (PTCL) of their world paths for ~48 hours** — both swinging onto Hurricane
> Electric on exactly 1–2 Jul, at a magnitude 2–8× larger than confirmed non-SMW5 control
> operators (Exp 6.1.1, W3b). Users rode out the fault on unchanged routes; the duopoly absorbed
> it upstream.

Combined with Exp 06 (data plane: congestion and jitter, no transit change), the event is now
characterised in **both planes** — consistent, complementary, from independent measurement
systems.

## The two-leg picture

**Leg 1 — local ISPs → the gates (baseline 26–30 Jun → fault day):** frozen. The one exception:
**Fasttel** swapped its majority from PTCL (0.74 → 0.46) to TWA (0.26 → 0.54) for ~2 days,
reverting by 3 Jul. Its shift began **1 Jul, a day before the PTA announcement** — evidence the
fault was operative before it was reported (Exp 06 inferred the same), *not* of foreknowledge.
**Independent confirmation attempted (Exp 6.1.1, W5):** country-level outage-detection signals
(IODA: BGP visibility, active-probing reachability) do not show a distinguishable anomaly before
2 Jul — expected, since those instruments catch blackouts and this was a degradation, not a
blackout (Exp 06). A latency-based cross-check (RIPE Atlas's own worldwide anchoring mesh, pinging
Pakistan's two anchors continuously and independent of our infrastructure) is in progress.

**Leg 2 — TWA → the world's carriers (unfiltered data):**

| TWA's carriers | before | **2 Jul** | after |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Cogent | 16% | **4%** ⬇ | 16% |
| Sparkle | 8.5% | 4.7% ⬇ | 7.4% |
| **Hurricane Electric** | 2% | **20.5%** ⬆ | 2.3% |

**Leg 2 — PTCL → the world:** same pattern, smaller magnitudes (PTCL reaches the world mostly by
peering, so its transit dependency is diffuse): **Hurricane 0% → 14.2% on exactly 1–2 Jul → 0%**,
Etisalat 2.1% → 7.4%, Arelion spiking 7.1% on the 2nd; Level3's decline began pre-fault (not
attributed). Both operators side-by-side: `results/fig_operators_upstreams.png`.

Route churn (fraction of world paths changing carrier vs baseline): TWA 2.1% → **20.9%** → 3.5%;
PTCL 11.9% (already elevated 1 Jul) → 13.5% → 3.9%. **Robustness (Exp 6.1.1, placebo-tested):**
a churn-magnitude claim was tested against four control operators, including two (Nepal, Vietnam)
with zero SMW5 exposure — all showed the same elevated churn *ranking* on 1–3 Jul, even after
correcting for a global IHR data gap on those days, so **churn magnitude is not attributable to
Pakistan or SMW5 and the claim is retracted.** What the same placebo test *confirms*: the size of
the swing onto Hurricane Electric specifically — TWA +9.1 pp, PTCL +7.1 pp, SLT (an SMW5 landing
country) +13.2 pp — is 2–8× larger than the two confirmed non-SMW5 controls (+1.5 pp, +3.8 pp).
Attribution rests on this carrier-specific, placebo-tested fingerprint, not on churn size.
(Separately, the *single largest* churn spike in TWA/PTCL's own 8-week series falls not on the
fault days but on **30–31 May**, decomposing as a one-day Hurricane-interconnect loss coincident
with a severe Lahore storm/power event — a candidate real-world cause, not placebo-tested but
methodologically distinct from the July contamination; Exp 6.1.1.)

## Verification & honesty ledger

All operator-level numbers re-verified on an **unfiltered** pull (the first pull dropped values
< 0.02, which can fake "appearances"). One sub-claim was **retracted** in the process (a "new"
PTCL→Cogent link — it pre-existed below the filter). A second, larger retraction followed a
placebo test (Exp 6.1.1, W3b): IHR's archive gaps on 2–3 Jul (45/96, 23/96 bins) turned out to be
**global, not Pakistan-specific**, and even after correcting for them, four control operators —
including two with zero SMW5 exposure — showed the same elevated churn ranking on those days. The
churn-*magnitude* claim was retracted as a result; the surviving evidence is the carrier-specific
Hurricane-swing comparison, tested against the same controls. Hegemony counts **paths, not
traffic**.

## Physical-cable connection (stated carefully)

Hegemony sees logical (BGP) routes, not fibre. The mechanism linking them: carrier interconnects
ride leased capacity on specific submarine systems; the fault killed the lanes under some
interconnects → their announcements withdrew → the world re-selected paths via surviving
interconnects → the shares moved. The 2 Jul churn spike is the **BGP shadow of the physical
failure** — attribution by the placebo-tested Hurricane-swing fingerprint (both operators onto the
same substitute carrier, at a magnitude 2–8× larger than confirmed non-SMW5 controls) plus
mechanism, since no public carrier↔cable mapping exists and churn magnitude alone is not
attributable (Exp 6.1.1, W3b). Obtaining that mapping (asks itemised in
the experiment notes; route: Nowmay/AINTEC'24) upgrades this to verification and enables a
quantified single-point-of-failure map per cable.
