## Two distinct RTT jumps on the Karachi probe

The RTT does not rise once — there are **two separate steps**, and they have
completely different causes:

| Step                                 | Hop                                              | RTT                      | Cause                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Access first-mile floor** | 1 → 2 (`192.168.10.1` → `39.39.0.1`, PTCL) | 0.8 →**~26 ms**   | The probe's own last-mile / CGNAT link. Present on**every** destination. |
| **2. International exit**      | **6–7**, at the first foreign hop         | **+77 to +116 ms** | Traffic physically leaves Pakistan into foreign transit.                       |

The first step is a **constant ~26 ms tax on everything this probe measures** — its
first PTCL hop (`39.39.0.1`) is already at 25 ms. This is unique to this probe: the
access floor on every *other* probe in the run is 0.5–4.5 ms (see comparison table).
**When quoting absolute RTTs for ptcl.khi, subtract ~26 ms to compare it to the
other vantages.** The second step is the real "exit Pakistan" event and is the jump
you observed.

**Observations**

- **The jump is geography, not congestion.** It sits at the PK→foreign hop and is
  stable within ±a few ms across all 48 h / ~190 rounds per site — consistent with
  the run-wide "no diurnal cycle, no route flapping" result.
- **PTCL has no single international upstream — it fans out per destination** across
  **NTT (AS2914), Colt/Level3 (AS3356), Cogent (AS174), Singtel (AS7473), Tata
  (AS6453)**. That is classic LDI multi-homing; PTCL carries its own transit.
- **Geo TV is the exception that proves the rule:** it's a Cloudflare zone (US ASN)
  but is **served from a local Cloudflare PoP at 26 ms** — PTCL hands off to
  Cloudflare at hop 7 (`221.120.200.3`, PTCL) with *no* RTT jump. Real local CDN
  peering. (Same ICMP-vs-HTTP caveat as Exp 01: 26 ms proves the *edge* is local,
  not that the HTTP `colo` is — worth a `/cdn-cgi/trace` confirmation.)
- The Lahore anchor reaches the same destinations over **different transit** and
  generally worse (NADRA via Akamai-NL ~300 ms; HBL via Incapsula ~228 ms), but its
  path is invisible so only its endpoint RTT is usable.

## Does PTCL peer with Transworld? — **Yes locally, no internationally**

Fraction of `ptcl.khi` paths whose route traverses Transworld (AS38193 / RDAP "TWA"):

| Destination class                             | via Transworld              |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Local (PK)** dests — Dunya News, FBR | **380 / 380 = 100 %** |
| **Abroad** dests (8 sites)              | **0 / 1520 = 0 %**    |

- **Local:** every PTCL-Karachi path to another Pakistani network hands off to
  **Transworld at `119.63.137.82` / `119.63.137.48` (~26–45 ms, in-country)**. PTCL
  uses **Transworld as its domestic interconnect to reach other PK ISPs** (e.g.
  Dunya News on Multinet: `17557 → 38193 → 9260`). This is the **private bilateral
  PTCL↔Transworld arrangement** noted in `CLAUDE.md`, and it is **domestic-only**.
- **Abroad:** Transworld appears in **zero** of the 1,520 international paths. PTCL
  does **not** transit Transworld for foreign traffic — it uses its own upstreams
  (NTT/Colt/Cogent/Singtel/Tata). PTCL is an LDI in its own right.

**Caveats**

- The handoff is a **private interconnect** — nothing here proves it goes through
  **PKIX** (no peering-LAN hop is visible). Treat it as PTCL↔Transworld bilateral.
- `ptcl.lhe` shows 0 % via Transworld for *local* sites too, **but that is an
  artifact of ICMP filtering** (its middle hops are invisible), **not** evidence
  that Lahore-PTCL avoids Transworld. The 100 % figure is from Karachi only.

## Verification: direct PTCL↔Transworld probe-to-probe test (2026-06-19)

A claim was raised that **PTCL and Transworld do not peer, so any PTCL↔TWA traffic
is routed internationally.** The 48 h analysis above only showed PTCL reaching TWA
*customers* (Multinet, FBR) — not TWA's own address space — and couldn't see the
reverse direction. So we ran the **decisive test**: traceroute + ping in **both
directions** between a PTCL endpoint and a Transworld endpoint, each being a real
probe's own egress IP **inside** that ISP. Script:
`experiments/02_isp_classification/ptcl_peering.py peering`
(results in `results/ptcl_peering/peering_routes.txt`).

**Decision rule (speed-of-light floor):** Karachi/Lahore → Singapore is ~60–90 ms,
→ Europe ~100–130 ms minimum. Any PTCL↔TWA RTT well under ~50 ms with all-PK hops
is *physically impossible* to have left the country.

| Direction | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **TWA(Lhe) → PTCL(Lhe)** `203.135.63.50` | **ping 1.5 ms**, 3 hops, all PK, reaches AS17557 directly | **local interconnect** |
| **TWA(Lhe) → PTCL(Khi)** `39.39.48.53` | **ping 47 ms** (3/3), reaches AS17557 Karachi | **domestic** (Lhe↔Khi distance) |
| **PTCL(Khi) → TWA** `110.93.235.222` | crosses at `119.63.137.82` → into **AS38193 at ~42 ms**, every hop PK | **domestic** |
| PTCL(Lhe) → TWA | ICMP-filtered (`* * *`); ping blocked | inconclusive (covered by reverse) |

**Verdict: the claim is FALSE.** PTCL and Transworld interconnect **domestically**.
A **1.5 ms** RTT between a Transworld host and a PTCL host in Lahore cannot exist
without a local interconnect (it implies near-same-facility). The Karachi PTCL probe
reaches *into Transworld's own AS38193* at ~42 ms via the `119.63.137.x` handoff —
the same handoff seen carrying the 48 h local-site traffic. Both directions stay in
Pakistan; nothing hairpins abroad.

**Nuances**
- **Routing is asymmetric** (as expected): PTCL→TWA goes via the `119.63.137.x`
  interconnect (~42 ms into Karachi-side TWA), while TWA→PTCL in Lahore is a direct
  3-hop 1.5 ms path. Different routes, both domestic.
- The pings *from* PTCL *to* the TWA probe IP did not return (that probe's egress
  drops ICMP echo), but the traceroute reaching AS38193 plus the returning reverse
  pings confirm the link in both directions.
- This still does **not** prove the interconnect is **PKIX** vs a private bilateral —
  no peering-LAN hop is visible. The most likely reading remains the **private
  PTCL↔Transworld arrangement**. But "routed internationally" is firmly disproved.

## Cross-check: reaching PTCL-hosted sites from every probe (2026-06-19)

Exp 01 found exactly two genuinely PTCL-hosted (AS17557) sites: **lums.edu.pk**
(`203.135.62.24`) and **ptcl.com.pk** (`221.120.226.61`). We traced to both from
all 8 probes — the Exp 02 "probe-on-A → host-on-B" test for PTCL as the *destination*
network. Script: `ptcl_peering.py hosted`; routes in
`results/ptcl_peering/hosted_routes.txt`. (Cybernet-Haripur was offline.)

**Every ISP reaches PTCL's servers domestically — no path leaves the country.**

| Probe → lums.edu.pk | Path to PTCL | RTT |
|---|---|---|
| Z-Com (Lhe) | via **Transworld** (`119.63.137.49`) → PTCL | 2.3 ms |
| Nova (Lhe) | Shaw-artifact → **Transworld** → PTCL | 4.2 ms |
| Nayatel (Isb) | via **Transworld** (`119.63.137.83`) → PTCL | 23 ms |
| Cybernet (Khi) | via **Paknet/PTCL** (`AS9557`) → PTCL | 22 ms |
| PTCL (Khi) | internal PTCL backbone | 42 ms |

`ptcl.com.pk` shows the same shape (every probe reaches PTCL's `116.71.241.234`
ingress in-country, 3–24 ms; the server firewalls its final hop, so blocks show
"no final response" but the path into PTCL is fully domestic).

**Third independent confirmation of the PTCL↔Transworld interconnect:** Z-Com, Nova
and Nayatel all reach PTCL-hosted content *through Transworld's `119.63.137.x`
handoff* — the same interconnect seen in the 48 h local-site traffic and the direct
peering test. Other ISPs ride the TWA↔PTCL link to reach PTCL, entirely in-country.

## Takeaways for the PKIX story

1. PTCL's domestic reach to other PK ISPs is **fast and in-country** — directly
   verified: PTCL↔Transworld interconnect locally (1.5–47 ms, never abroad). It goes
   through a **private PTCL↔Transworld bilateral**, *not* a visible IXP — reinforcing
   that inter-ISP traffic in PK is settled bilaterally through the two LDIs rather
   than at PKIX.
2. The **offshore penalty is a hard +80–200 ms step** at a single hop, identical in
   shape on every ISP — a hosting/peering problem, not capacity. PTCL spreads it
   across five foreign transit providers but the penalty is the same.
3. **Local CDN peering works when it exists** (Geo TV at 26 ms on PTCL Karachi) —
   the lever is getting more content onto local PoPs / PKIX, not more bandwidth.
