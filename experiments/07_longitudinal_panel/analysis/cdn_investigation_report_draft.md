What Better Cloudflare Access Would Actually Be Worth

**Building the CDN picture from existing data**

I pulled the already collected panel data and built a full matrix of every internet service provider against every content delivery network hosted site, using real per pair numbers rather than averages. This showed that thirty three of the forty content delivery network sites in the sample were actually Cloudflare, with only a handful on Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and Sucuri, and that those non Cloudflare sites were uniformly slow for every single internet service provider, including the best performing ones. I then read the actual hop by hop traceroute paths to work out the mechanism behind each internet service provider's Cloudflare performance. I found that Cybernet mostly handed traffic directly to Cloudflare on its own network, that Zcom, Nova, and Orbit were all riding Transworld's backbone through a Hong Kong detour, and that PTCL split roughly evenly between a direct local path and a paid international transit path through a company called Level3, depending on which specific Cloudflare address range a site used.

**Checking whether direct looking paths were really local**

I checked whether every one of Cybernet's seemingly direct Cloudflare paths was actually local. A minority of them had round trip times too slow to be confirmed local under the project's physics floor. However, following the same standard the paper itself uses for its main tromboning detector, I do not treat round trip time alone as proof that a path left the country, since domestic congestion can look the same as a real foreign detour. I checked these specific traces for an independently confirmed foreign hop and did not find one. I am flagging these cases as unresolved rather than claiming them one way or the other.

**Checking the peering evidence properly**

Since PeeringDB is self reported and not proof of anything by itself, I moved to checking real internet routing data instead. I queried PeeringDB for every internet service provider and every major content delivery network to see which ones share any internet exchange, then verified the most important case using a live routing table looking glass operated by DE CIX for the Karachi exchange. This confirmed that PTCL and a content delivery network called ACE CDN, run by Tencent, both have active working connections at the same Karachi exchange right now. Earlier project work had already shown that PTCL's actual traffic to that same content delivery network does not use this shared connection and instead goes through a private link. This is now the strongest, most fully proven finding in the whole project, since it is confirmed independently both by live routing data and by measured traffic.

**Quantifying what better Cloudflare access would be worth**

Since Cloudflare covers the large majority of the sampled content delivery network sites, I calculated what better access would actually be worth for every internet service provider currently performing worse than Cybernet. Every worse performing internet service provider would see a real latency improvement, ranging from about a quarter faster up to about three quarters faster, if it could match Cybernet's average Cloudflare performance. I also want to note a distinction I clarified along the way, that asking one internet service provider to share its private advantage with a competitor is not a realistic request, and that the cleaner recommendation is for content delivery networks to place shared infrastructure at the exchange, following the precedent already set by the PTCL and ACE CDN case.

**Testing directly from inside the networks**

To get a fully confirmed answer about where Cloudflare actually serves content from, rather than an inferred one, I logged directly into five of the project's own Raspberry Pi probes over their private network connection, and confirmed each one's identity by matching its public internet address to the official probe records. This covered Nova, two Cybernet locations, and two PTCL locations. I then ran a direct web request from each of these five computers to a diagnostic address that Cloudflare provides, which reveals the exact city that actually answered the request.

Two of the five test sites came back as genuinely served from Karachi for every one of the five connections tested, including internet service providers that otherwise perform poorly on other sites. Two other test sites came back as served from Singapore for every one of the five connections, with no exceptions. One site was split, with most internet service providers reaching Singapore but both PTCL connections instead reaching a Cloudflare location in Muscat, Oman, which matched PTCL's own confirmed international exchange connections found earlier.

To understand why every internet service provider reached Karachi so easily for those two sites, I ran actual hop by hop traceroutes from the same five computers to those two destinations, rather than just the web request test. This showed a clear, consistent mechanism rather than an individual peering deal per customer. Cybernet routes both of its tested locations, Haripur and Karachi, to one single identified router on its own network before handing off directly to Cloudflare. PTCL routes both of its tested locations, Mianwali and Karachi, to one single identified router whose own name directly references Karachi's exchange facility, before handing off to Cloudflare, most likely its own private connection rather than the shared exchange itself, consistent with the same pattern already confirmed for PTCL and ACE CDN. Within each of these two internet service providers separately, every tested customer is funneled through that one same domestic connection point regardless of which city they are actually in, which is why both locations tested for a given internet service provider got the same result. The two internet service providers each have their own separate router, not a shared one. I was not able to run this same check for Nova, since that computer does not have the tool needed installed, and I have no access at all inside any other internet service provider, so this pattern is confirmed only for Cybernet and PTCL, not shown to be true of Pakistani internet service providers generally.

One of these results directly contradicted the original week long measurement from July, where Cybernet had performed poorly on one of these same sites. The fresh test now showed that same site as genuinely local for Cybernet. I concluded that Cloudflare's internal routing for that particular site had most likely changed sometime in the month between the two measurements.

**Understanding why results differ by site and by internet service provider**

Different websites can give different answers even from the same internet service provider because Cloudflare assigns each customer to a specific address range, and each address range is only announced from a limited set of Cloudflare's data centers, so some content simply is not available locally no matter how good an internet service provider's connections are. The same website can also give different answers to different internet service providers, because when the same address range is announced from more than one data center, each internet service provider's own network connections determine which announcement looks closest to it. This is why PTCL specifically ended up in Oman while other providers ended up in Singapore for the same website.

**Extending the test with Globalping**

Since my own computers only cover five internet service providers, I found a separate, free, publicly available service called Globalping, built by the team behind the jsDelivr content delivery network, which allows the same kind of direct web request test from volunteer run computers inside Pakistan that I have no direct access to otherwise.

I scaled this up specifically for Nayatel across all thirty three Cloudflare hosted sites already in the sample. Thirty one of the thirty three tests succeeded, and every single one came back as served from Islamabad, a Pakistani city that had not appeared anywhere else in the data before. This is the standout result of the whole investigation. Unlike the Karachi result above, this was not just a case of Nayatel matching what other internet service providers already got. For the two sites where every other tested internet service provider was stuck going all the way to Singapore, Nayatel still reached a Pakistani location. To confirm this was not simply trusting Cloudflare's own claim, I also checked the actual connection timing returned by the test itself, and found the raw network handshake time was consistently under three milliseconds across all thirty one results, independently confirming genuine physical closeness. Nayatel is, by a clear margin, the most consistently and most thoroughly confirmed locally served internet service provider in the entire project, though I have not yet identified the mechanism behind it the way I did for PTCL and ACE CDN.

I also tried the same scaled test on four other Pakistani networks available on Globalping. These results were far less consistent, and I traced this back to a platform limitation where a single network name on Globalping can be answered by more than one physical connection, sometimes run by the same volunteer across multiple listed networks. Because of this, I am not presenting those four networks' results with the same confidence as Nayatel's.

**Contributions this work can make to the paper**

Cloudflare's own confirmed answer that Nayatel reaches an Islamabad based Cloudflare location for the overwhelming majority of tested sites, including sites where every other tested internet service provider was stuck going abroad, giving the paper a definitive, directly confirmed locality claim for its best performing internet service provider.

The PTCL and ACE CDN case as the paper's strongest example of an exchange being technically available and confirmed active, and still not used, since it is independently confirmed both by live routing data and by measured traffic.

A corrected, Cloudflare specific savings table showing real percentage improvements per internet service provider if it matched Cybernet's average performance.

A clear mechanistic explanation, backed by real traceroute paths, for why different internet service providers get different Cloudflare performance, distinguishing genuine local peering from inherited transit paths and from a per site anycast effect.

Direct confirmation, with named routers, that Cybernet and PTCL each have their own separate single domestic connection point to Cloudflare, and that each one funnels all of its own tested customers nationwide through that one point, rather than through individual per city or per customer arrangements. This is confirmed only for these two internet service providers, since they are the only ones I currently have direct access inside, not shown to be true of Pakistani internet service providers generally.

Evidence that Cloudflare's routing for the same site can change within a single month, worth noting as a caveat on any single measurement week.

Confirmation that Pakistan has more than one Cloudflare location, Karachi and Islamabad both confirmed from multiple independent sources, which was not previously documented in the paper. A possible third location in Lahore came up but only from a source I later found to be unreliable, so it is a lead worth checking further rather than a confirmed fact.
